TechQuest 30
What if innovation had consequences?

Most hackathons reward whoever builds the best thing. But the best thing for whom? And for how long?

The real world doesn't grade you on your prototype. It grades you on what happens after — the ripple effects, the trade-offs, the unintended consequences. A self-driving car fleet might solve urban congestion and wipe out 3 million jobs. An AI diagnostic tool could save lives in cities and deepen healthcare inequality in rural areas.

Great innovation isn't just about solving problems. It's about knowing which new problems you're creating.

The concept

Monopoly, but it's the future — and you're building it.

Teams receive a pool of Innovation Credits and invest them into solving real-world challenges. Every decision feeds into a shared digital ecosystem — a living simulation of a virtual society. What you build affects what everyone else is working with.

Unlike Monopoly, winning isn't about owning the most. It's about building something that actually lasts — economically, technologically, and socially.

How it works

Every solution alters the shared simulation. A manufacturing breakthrough might spike economic output but tank carbon scores. An AI health tool might boost welfare while triggering a regulation crisis. At unpredictable intervals, global events drop into the game — forcing every team to adapt:

Energy crisis
Cyberattack wave
New AI regulations
Scientific breakthrough

The team that built the most efficient solution last round might find it obsolete — or dangerous — this round.

The scoreboard

Teams are judged across four dimensions simultaneously. Optimise only for profit? Watch your planet score tank. Chase sustainability at all costs? The economy crumbles.

For 30 years, Techfest has celebrated what technology can do. TechQuest 30 asks the harder question — what should it do, and at what cost? Innovation isn't about owning the board. It's about being brave enough to shape it responsibly.
